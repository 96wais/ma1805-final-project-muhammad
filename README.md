## Overview of the Game
Burnout ride is an interactive p5.js arcade-style game that explores the theme of overworking and mental burnout. The player controls a speeding bike moving through a stressful environment filled with obstacles, visual distortion, and escalating pressure systems such as “stress,” “overthinking,” and “digital overload.” The core idea is to represent burnout not as a story, but physically experiencing it whilst you play. the mroe you play, the more you get more anxious about dying as your going faster, as well as more obstacles in the way.

## Creativity 
This project goes beyond a simple runner game by transforming emotional and mental states into a physical game people can play so they experience what burnout is. Burnout reflects on several occasions, not just work. The game i created reflects on that. Some features i added also causes stress and burnout such as:

- Stress increases dynamically based on speed
- Visual effects (shake, vignette, sky colour) reflect mental pressure
- “Extra systems” like fake phone battery, inbox overload, and overthinking meter simulate digital burnout
- Glitch words and neon signs create a layered sense of cognitive overload

Instead of focusing purely on score or survival, the game attempts to simulate psychological terror, making the experience expressive.

I pushed beyond basic tutorial-level p5.js by combining:
- physics-based movement (jumping + acceleration)
- procedural difficulty scaling
- multi-layered environmental systems
- reactive UI tied directly to player behaviour

##  Criticality / Reflection

### What worked well:
- The stress system successfully creates a sense of escalation, i felt it so hopefully others aswell
- Visual feedback (shake, colour shift, glitch effects) really affects the gameplay and how the player is feeling 
- Jump mechanic significantly improved playability and skill expression, this was added at the end of the game and gave a huge difference

### What didn’t work initially:
- Early versions lacked player control (no jump, only speed control)
- Collision detection was too harsh and didn’t account for jumping properly.
- Hitbox (the red blocks) was too big, meaning that player would die even though the player didnt touch the killlblock

### Improvements made:
- Added jumping to introduce skill-based gameplay and tenstion
- Balanced obstacle spawning to improve fairness, players had time to know what to do
- Linked more systems to gameplay outcomes rather than visuals alone

### What I learned:
- Game feel depends more on feelings than complexity
- Too many systems can overwhelm unless they are clearly connected
- Small adjustments to physics (gravity, acceleration) have a large impact on player experience

I also learned how important debugging structure is in p5.js projects.
